The Canson Surplus Case

In 2017, a $474,184.38 foreclosure surplus owed to Patricia Byrd Canson, then 74, went into her lawyer Donald Iwuchukwu's trust account. She died in 2023 without having been paid any of it. Her lawyer has since given four different accounts of where the money went, and a trust naming him trustee is under challenge as forged.

In short

  • Documented The surplus check, payable to Ms. Canson, was endorsed "Patricia Byrd Canson (POA)" and deposited into the Law Offices of Donald Iwuchukwu account at Citibank on March 7, 2017.
  • Documented Two days later his office sent her a cashier's check for $204,347.50, "minus the hold-back (per instruction) and fees of 10%." That left $269,836.88 withheld. About $222,000 of it has never been accounted for.
  • Documented In April 2018 Mr. Iwuchukwu had the cashier's check cancelled and reissued. The replacement was cashed on July 16, 2018. Ms. Canson still had the original check when she died, and listed it as an asset of her 2023 trust.
  • Documented Mr. Iwuchukwu has given four inconsistent explanations of what happened to the $204,347.50. The man he says received it swears he never did.
  • Court action On April 14, 2026, the probate court suspended Mr. Iwuchukwu as trustee of the disputed 2017 Trust and issued its own order to show cause regarding sanctions.
  • Alleged Mr. Smith says the 2017 Trust and the replacement-check endorsement are forgeries. A sanctions motion seeking $3.46 million was heard on November 12, 2026 and continued without a ruling.

The money

Ms. Canson's late husband, Dr. Clarence Canson, owned a house at 10 Powder Bowl Court. It was sold at a trustee's sale on November 29, 2016, leaving a surplus after the lender was paid. That surplus belonged to Ms. Canson as his successor.

(Depo. 156; Aurin is inconsistent on some details, such as how often he met Mr. Iwuchukwu)

The 2017 Trust

Mr. Iwuchukwu relies on a trust document dated February 28, 2017. It names him initial trustee, with no bond, and leaves Ms. Canson's estate 80% to Aurin Canson, 10% to her friend Sheila Webb and 10% to a "Sue Bochac." In August 2023, days before her death, Ms. Canson signed a different trust before a notary and witnesses after a doctor found she had capacity. It leaves her estate to 19 relatives and friends.

Problems with the 2017 Trust

  • Documented The notary whose stamp appears on it testified she did not notarize it. Her journal has no entry for it.
  • Documented Mr. Iwuchukwu signed his acceptance as trustee on January 20, 2023, in Los Angeles. That is nearly six years after the trust's date and seven months before Ms. Canson died.
  • Documented Nobody in Ms. Canson's life has heard of "Sue Bochac." The only Bohac in the record is a 2021 foreclosure-surplus payee whose check also went into Mr. Iwuchukwu's account under a "(POA)" endorsement. If the name came from that 2021 file, the document could not have been drafted in 2017.
  • Documented Sheila Webb, who would inherit 10% under it, testifies against it.
  • Alleged A 2005 "Clarence Canson trust" his office gave the foreclosure trustee in 2016 bears what a handwriting examiner calls a "crude simulation" of Dr. Canson's signature, with his middle name misspelled.

Points that cut the other way

  • The notary's journal does show Ms. Canson signing an Indemnity Agreement before her on February 23, 2017, five days before the trust's date. She did visit this notary that week.
  • The notary left her stamp and journal at her workplace overnight and said the signature "looks very similar" to hers. That shows the acknowledgment is false. It does not by itself show who made it.
  • No handwriting expert has yet examined Ms. Canson's signature on the 2017 Trust or on the 2018 check endorsement.
  • The bank account that received the July 2018 replacement check has not yet been identified in the record.

  • Documented Large deposits were often followed within days by checks of roughly 15–20% of the deposit to Mr. Iwuchukwu or his firm. In one case a $492,085 surplus was followed two days later by $98,400 to his law group.
  • Documented The records show many cash withdrawals of $500 to $15,000. Only one direct payout to a client appears, for 67% of that client's deposit.
  • Alleged Other former clients have filed State Bar complaints or lawsuits alleging withheld settlement money, work not done, and false advertising. These have not been decided. Ms. Armstrong's own State Bar complaint about his websites (Aug. 26, 2026) is posted.

These records show a high-volume surplus-recovery practice with large, fast fee draws. They do not by themselves show whether other clients were paid what they were owed; that would take per-client ledgers or interviews. Client names and account numbers are omitted here.
