Beyond the Canson Case

Ms. Canson's surplus is not the only money that court filings say went missing around attorney Donald Iwuchukwu and his frequent co-counsel Metu Ogike. Lawsuits and State Bar complaints from at least nine households describe foreclosure surpluses that never reached the owner, "loans" that were never secured or repaid, and client trust funds held back. The amounts alleged come to more than $2 million.

Mr. McMullen’s later appeals fail in 2019 and 2020.

    Rosas I (Apr. 14, 2017, B270721); McMullen I (Mar. 26, 2019, B286505); Rosas v. Kensington (Dec. 29, 2020, B299784)

What authorities have done

  • Since March 2025, Ms. Armstrong has filed reports with the Contra Costa DA (No. 013-1139031), the Los Angeles and Orange County DAs, the FTC (No. 192145463), the FBI, the U.S. Department of Justice, the OCC, the California Attorney General and the State Bar. She says the responses have called these civil matters for now (Armstrong Decl. ¶135).
  • Ms. Armstrong says the State Bar has previously closed complaints from Ms. Brin, Mr. Choe, Mr. Mooney, Ms. Kim, Ms. Oh and herself (Armstrong Decl. ¶165).
  • Two courts have ruled against the group on the merits so far: the 2016 order in the Mooney matter directing Mr. Iwuchukwu and Mr. Ogike to return the seized funds (left standing on appeal in 2017), and the October 2025 denial of their anti-SLAPP motions in the Canson civil case.
